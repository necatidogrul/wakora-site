Honest comparison

Wakora vs Alarmy

By Necati Dogrul, developer of Wakora · 4 August 2026

I build Wakora, so treat this page accordingly. I have tried to make it useful rather than flattering, which means saying plainly where Alarmy is the better choice. If you only read one line: Alarmy is the safer pick if you want a mature, feature-dense app with a generous free tier, and Wakora is the better pick if you want a cheaper subscription and an alarm that is stricter by default.

Where Alarmy is genuinely better

Alarmy has been shipping for years and it shows. Its free tier is wider than mine: you get several missions, sleep tracking and sleep sounds without paying. It has a far larger rating base, which matters if you want the reassurance of a product thousands of people have already stress-tested. It supports more languages, and its sleep-tracking side is a real feature rather than an afterthought.

If your problem is "I want a well-known alarm app with lots of options and I do not want to pay", Alarmy is the more sensible download and I would not argue otherwise.

Where Wakora is different

The subscription is roughly a third cheaper per year. The snooze model is stricter by design: each snooze costs more than the last rather than simply being limited, which changes the incentive rather than the count.

The post-dismiss wake-up check is the piece I have not seen elsewhere. Two minutes after you dismiss the alarm, Wakora asks whether you are still up. The common failure mode with mission alarms is doing the mission half-awake and then going straight back to bed — that check exists specifically to catch it.

The honest summary: Alarmy wins on maturity, free-tier breadth and language coverage. Wakora wins on price and on the strictness of the snooze and post-dismiss design. Neither is a medical device and neither can guarantee you wake up.

What both apps cannot do

Neither app can override a phone that is switched off, out of battery, or has notifications fully revoked. Neither is a substitute for going to bed earlier. Both are alarm apps with friction added; the friction helps, but the underlying sleep debt is yours.

A note on the name

Wakora was published as "WakeGuard" until August 2026 and was renamed following a trademark claim from an unrelated developer using the same name. It is the same app, same developer, and existing subscriptions and streaks carried over.
