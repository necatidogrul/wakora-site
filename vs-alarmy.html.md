# Wakora vs Alarmy — an honest comparison of mission alarm apps

By Necati Dogrul, developer of Wakora · 4 August 2026 · https://wakora.app/vs-alarmy.html

> Disclosure: written by the developer of one of the two apps. Sections where the competing app
> is stronger are stated explicitly.

**Short answer:** Alarmy is the safer pick if you want a mature, feature-dense app with a
generous free tier. Wakora is the better pick if you want a cheaper subscription and an alarm
that is stricter by default.

## At a glance

| | Wakora | Alarmy |
|---|---|---|
| App Store ratings (US) | Newly renamed, small rating base | ~241,000 |
| Yearly subscription (US) | $39.99 | $59.99–$69.99 |
| Free trial | 7 days on the yearly plan | Varies by offer |
| Free tier | Narrower — math, shake, steps | Wider — several missions, sleep tracking, sounds |
| Missions | 20, of which 8 require leaving the bed | Comparable range, plus photo and squat modes |
| Snooze model | Escalating penalty with a daily cap | Configurable limits |
| Post-dismiss check | Yes, two minutes after dismissal | No direct equivalent |
| Languages | 10 | More |

## Where Alarmy is genuinely better

Alarmy's free tier is wider: several missions, sleep tracking and sleep sounds without paying.
It has a far larger rating base and supports more languages, and its sleep-tracking side is a
real feature rather than an afterthought. If the goal is "a well-known alarm app with lots of
options, without paying", Alarmy is the more sensible download.

## Where Wakora is different

The subscription is roughly a third cheaper per year. The snooze model is stricter by design:
each snooze costs more than the last rather than simply being capped, which changes the
incentive rather than the count.

The post-dismiss wake-up check is the distinguishing feature. Two minutes after dismissal Wakora
asks whether you are still up. The common failure mode with mission alarms is completing the
mission half-awake and returning to bed; that check exists to catch it.

## What neither app can do

Neither can override a phone that is switched off, out of battery, or has notifications revoked.
Neither is a medical device and neither can guarantee you wake up. Both add friction to an alarm;
the friction helps, but the underlying sleep debt is yours.

## Name history

Wakora was published as "WakeGuard" until August 2026 and was renamed following a trademark claim
from an unrelated developer using the same name. Same app, same developer; subscriptions and
streaks carried over.
