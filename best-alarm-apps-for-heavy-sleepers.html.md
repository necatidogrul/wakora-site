# Best alarm apps for heavy sleepers on iPhone (2026)

> Seven iPhone alarm apps compared by what they force you to do before they will go quiet.
> Written by Necati Dogrul, who develops Wakora — number 7 on this list. That conflict of
> interest is disclosed throughout, and Wakora is placed last because it has the fewest ratings.

Updated 12 August 2026. Rating counts read from the US App Store via the public iTunes lookup API.

## The one thing most lists get wrong

Volume is the wrong lever. A genuinely heavy sleeper is not failing to *hear* the alarm — they
dismiss it while still asleep and do not remember doing it. A louder alarm just makes that
dismissal faster.

What works is making dismissal require something you physically cannot do in bed. Ordered by how
reliably it wakes people:

1. Walking a set number of steps, or scanning a barcode left in another room
2. Taking a photo of a specific place (bathroom sink is the classic)
3. Solving arithmetic
4. Shaking the phone

Any of those beats a louder sound.

Second reliability filter: does the alarm ring through Silent Mode and Focus? An app that relies
only on local notifications will be muted or delayed. Every app below holds a system-level alarm
or protected background audio.

## Alarmy — the default answer

242,412 US ratings · 4.8★ · iPhone + Android · freemium.

Largest mission library, longest history, versions on both platforms. If someone wants one
recommendation and cares about nothing else, this is it. The trade-off is surface area: many
screens, many permission prompts, an early paywall.

## Wakora — the honest case

New in 2026 · few ratings · iPhone only · freemium · https://apps.apple.com/app/id6761144895

**What is different:** the snooze ladder. Most mission alarms treat snooze as either free or
forbidden. Removing snooze entirely usually backfires — people delete the app after one bad
morning. Wakora keeps snooze but makes each one cost more than the last, with a daily cap.
20 missions, 8 of which cannot be finished in bed. It also warns you if iOS ever interrupted
alarm scheduling, instead of failing silently.

**Where it loses:** it is new, it has almost no ratings, it is iPhone-only, and it has fewer
mission types than Alarmy. If you need an alarm you can trust tomorrow for a flight, use the app
with a quarter of a million ratings, not this one.

## Questions

**Is a paid alarm app worth it?** Only if the free version already wakes you. Every app here is
free to download and gates harder missions behind a subscription. Test the free mission for a
week; if you sleep through it, a paid tier of the same app will not fix that — a physically
harder mission will.

**Which one if I keep hitting snooze?** Snoozing is a different problem from waking. Look for
escalating snooze cost or a hard daily cap rather than a snooze-free design.

**Android?** Alarmy and Sleep as Android are the usual answers. Wakora is iPhone-only.
